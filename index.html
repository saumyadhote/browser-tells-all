This is not a measurement of how unique you are. It is a rough score based on how many identifying signals your browser exposes, weighted by how identifying each one tends to be (Low = 1, Medium = 2, High = 3 points). Real uniqueness depends on how many other people share your exact values, which can only be known by comparing against a large dataset.

    Why this matters

    Any single detail above is shared by millions of people. Combined, they can form a browser fingerprint: a set of values that is often distinctive enough to recognise the same browser across different visits and different websites.

    • It works without cookies. Clearing cookies or using a private window does not change most of these values.
    • It is invisible. There is no permission prompt and no banner. Any script on a page can read these values.
    • It is used for more than ads. Fraud detection and bot prevention also rely on fingerprints, which is one reason browsers cannot simply remove these APIs.

    How to reduce your fingerprint

    The goal is to look like many other people, not to hide everything. Unusual settings can make you stand out more.

    • Use a browser with fingerprinting protection. Tor Browser makes users look alike; Brave randomises some values; Firefox and Safari include built-in fingerprinting protections.
    • Keep your browser updated. Common, current versions blend in better than old or rare ones.
    • Install fewer extensions and avoid heavily customised fonts or settings. Every unusual choice adds information.
    • Use a reputable content blocker. Blocking known tracking scripts stops many of them from collecting a fingerprint in the first place.
    • Turn on Global Privacy Control if your browser supports it. In some places, such as California, sites are legally required to treat it as an opt-out of selling your data.
    • Remember the bigger picture. Your IP address is visible to every site you visit and is not shown here. A VPN or Tor hides it from the sites themselves.
