    How rare is your browser?

    Each signal is scored by how many people share your value. A value half of all users share is worth 1 bit; 1 in 1,000 is about 10 bits. Combine them and you get how many browsers it takes before one looks exactly like yours.

        Why this matters

        Any single detail above is shared by millions of people. Combined, they form a browser fingerprint that can recognise the same browser across visits and across sites.

        • It works without cookies. Clearing cookies or using a private window does not change most of these values.
        • It is invisible. There is no permission prompt and no banner. Any script on any page can run the same inferences you just saw.
        • It is used for more than ads. Fraud detection and bot prevention also rely on fingerprints, which is one reason browsers cannot simply remove these APIs.

        How to reduce your fingerprint

        The goal is to look like many other people, not to hide everything. Unusual settings can make you stand out more.

        • Use a browser with fingerprinting protection. Tor Browser makes users look alike; Brave randomises some values; Firefox and Safari include built-in protections.
        • Keep your browser updated. Common, current versions blend in better than old or rare ones.
        • Install fewer extensions and avoid heavily customised fonts or settings. Every unusual choice adds information.
        • Use a reputable content blocker. Blocking known tracking scripts stops many of them from collecting a fingerprint at all.
        • Turn on Global Privacy Control if your browser supports it. In some places, such as California, sites must treat it as an opt-out.
        • Remember your IP address. It is visible to every site and is not shown here. A VPN or Tor hides it from the sites themselves.
